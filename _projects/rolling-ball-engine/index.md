## Overview

The Rolling Ball Engine is a fully machined mechanical engine inspired by the motion and cyclic behavior of a Stirling engine. I manufactured the engine components using Haas CNC mills and lathes, then assembled and tuned the mechanism until it ran smoothly.

This project focused on taking a mechanical concept from raw material to a working physical assembly, with special attention to machining strategy, surface finish, bearing alignment, and low-friction motion.


---

## Mechanical Design

The engine uses a rolling ball mechanism to shift mass and drive continuous motion through a rotating assembly. Like a Stirling engine, the design depends on a repeating cycle, careful timing, and smooth transfer of energy through the mechanism.

| Subsystem | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| Rolling ball assembly | Shifts mass through the engine cycle |
| Rotating frame | Supports the moving ball path and main motion |
| Shaft and bearing features | Keep the engine aligned and reduce friction |
| Machined base | Provides a rigid foundation for smooth operation |

---

## Manufacturing

I machined the engine using Haas CNC mills and lathes. The process included planning workholding, machining individual components, finishing critical features, and checking fits during assembly.

Key manufacturing work included:

- CNC milling the engine frame, base, and flat-sided components
- CNC turning round components on the Haas lathe
- Holding tight fits for shafts, bearings, and rotating interfaces
- Deburring and finishing parts to reduce friction
- Assembling and tuning the engine until the motion was smooth and repeatable
